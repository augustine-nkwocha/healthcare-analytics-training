# Stakeholder Summary

## 1. Project Overview

This analysis examined the relationship between household wealth and childhood stunting among children under five years of age using data from the 2018 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS).

The purpose of the analysis was to understand whether household wealth is associated with childhood stunting and to provide evidence that can support public health planning and decision-making.



## 2. Key Findings

- Overall childhood stunting prevalence was 36.16% among children with valid anthropometric measurements.

- Children from the poorest households had the highest stunting prevalence (54.98%), while children from the richest households had the lowest prevalence (15.73%).

- Stunting prevalence decreased consistently as household wealth increased.

- The distribution of Height-for-Age Z-scores indicated that most children were shorter for their age than the WHO reference population.



## 3. Why These Findings Matter

The findings indicate that childhood stunting remains an important public health challenge.

The observed relationship between household wealth and childhood stunting suggests that socioeconomic conditions are closely associated with child nutritional outcomes.

Targeting interventions toward populations experiencing the highest burden of stunting may improve programme effectiveness and contribute to better child health outcomes.



## 4. Recommended Actions

- Prioritize nutrition programmes in poorer communities.

- Strengthen maternal and child nutrition interventions.

- Continue routine monitoring of childhood growth indicators through national surveys and health information systems.

- Support multi-sector collaboration involving health, education, agriculture, water and sanitation, and social protection programmes.

- Conduct additional studies to investigate other determinants of childhood stunting.



## 5. Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates a clear inverse association between household wealth and childhood stunting among children under five years of age in Nigeria.

These findings support the need for targeted public health interventions aimed at reducing socioeconomic inequalities and improving child nutrition.

Continued monitoring and further analytical studies will strengthen evidence-based decision-making and contribute to improved child health outcomes.
