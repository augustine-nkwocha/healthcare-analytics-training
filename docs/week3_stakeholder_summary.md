# Background

The Behavioral Risk Factor Surveilance System (BRFSS) is a large U.S. population-based health survey that collects information on health conditions, health-related behaviors, and risk factors among adults. This analysis  focuses on diabetes prevalence and seleted demographic and behavioral characteristics among respondents in the 2022 BRFSS survey.

# Objective

To estimate diabetes prevalence among BRFSS 2022 respondents and assess how prevalence differs according to age group, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking  status, and physical activity status.

# Key Findings

1. Overall diabetes prevalence was 13.77% among respondents with known diabetes status.

2. Diabetes prevalence increased substantially with age, rising from 1.37% among respondents aged 18–24 years to 23.44% among those aged 75–79 years.

3. Obese respondents had a diabetes prevalence of 22.37%, compared with 5.88% among underweight respondents.

4. Physically inactive respondents had approximately double the diabetes prevalence of physically active respondents (22.52% vs 11.00%).

5. Former smokers had the highest diabetes prevalence among smoking categories (17.37%).

6. Differences by sex were relatively small, with prevalence of 14.49% among males and 13.14% among females.

# Public Health Implications

1. Older adults represent a high-risk population and may benefit from targeted diabetes screening programs.

2. Obesity appears strongly associated with diabetes prevalence and should remain a priority area for prevention and intervention efforts.

3. Promotion of physical activity may help reduce diabetes burden at the population level.

4. Diabetes prevention strategies should focus on modifiable risk factors while prioritizing high-risk demographic groups.

# Limitations

1. BRFSS is an observational cross-sectional survey and cannot establish causal relationships.

2. Survey responses are self-reported and may be subject to recall or reporting bias.

3. Some variables contained missing data, particularly BMI and smoking-related variables.

4. Findings represent associations and should not be interpreted as proof of causation.

# Conclusion

Diabetes prevalence in the BRFSS 2022 population was 13.77%. Age, BMI, and physical activity demonstrated the strongest associations with diabetes prevalence. These findings support continued focus on obesity prevention, physical activity promotion, and targeted screening among older adults as potential strategies for reducing diabetes burden.
