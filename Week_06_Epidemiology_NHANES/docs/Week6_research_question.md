# Research Question

## Background

Cigarette smoking is an important modifiable health behaviour associated
with several chronic diseases. Diabetes is also a major chronic health
condition and an important public health concern.

This project will use cross-sectional data from the National Health and
Nutrition Examination Survey to examine whether cigarette-smoking status
is associated with diabetes among adults in the United States.

## Rationale

Understanding whether cigarette smoking is associated with diabetes among
US adults can help identify population groups that may benefit from
targeted prevention, smoking cessation programmes, and chronic disease
control strategies.

The findings may also contribute to public health evidence regarding the
relationship between behavioural risk factors and diabetes prevalence.

## Primary Research Question

Among US adults represented in NHANES 2017–March 2020 pre-pandemic data,
is cigarette-smoking status associated with diabetes status?

## Population

Adults represented in the NHANES 2017–March 2020 pre-pandemic dataset who
have sufficient information for the exposure, outcome and selected
covariates.

The precise age threshold and eligibility criteria will be confirmed after
examining the available variables and their documentation.

## Exposure

Cigarette-smoking status.

The final exposure definition and categories will be determined after
reviewing the NHANES smoking variables, response options and missing-value
codes.

## Outcome

Diabetes status.

The final outcome definition will be determined after reviewing the
available NHANES diabetes variables and their coding.

## Primary Objective

To estimate and describe the association between cigarette-smoking status
and diabetes status among US adults represented in the selected NHANES
cycle.

## Secondary Objectives

1. To describe the characteristics of the eligible study population.
2. To estimate the prevalence of cigarette-smoking categories.
3. To estimate the prevalence of diabetes.
4. To compare diabetes prevalence across cigarette-smoking categories.
5. To examine whether the observed association differs after accounting
   for selected potential confounders.
6. To interpret the findings while recognising the limitations of
   cross-sectional observational data.

## Null Hypothesis

There is no association between cigarette-smoking status and diabetes
status among the study population.

## Alternative Hypothesis

There is an association between cigarette-smoking status and diabetes
status among the study population.

## Nature of the Inference

The analysis will investigate an association.

Because NHANES is observational and the proposed analysis is
cross-sectional, the study will not establish that cigarette smoking
causes diabetes.
