# Nigeria COVID-19 Surveillance Review (2020–2021)

## Objective

To examine COVID-19 surveillance data for Nigeria between 2020 and 2021 and identify major epidemic waves, mortality patterns, and data quality considerations.

## Data Source

WHO COVID-19 Global Daily Surveillance Dataset

Analysis focused on Nigeria and the period 2020–2021 because later years contained substantial missingness in daily case and death reporting.

## Data Quality Findings

- Date_reported was successfully converted to datetime format.
- Daily case and death reporting became increasingly incomplete after 2021.
- Missingness was substantially higher from 2022 onward.
- Cumulative indicators contained no missing values.
- Missingness appeared associated with reporting date and may reflect changes in surveillance reporting practices.
- Missing values were retained and documented rather than imputed.

## Epidemiologic Findings

Four epidemic waves were observed.

Wave 1:
- Approximate period: April 2020 to October 2020
- Peak: July 2020

Wave 2:
- Approximate period: December 2020 to April 2021
- Peak: January 2021

Wave 3:
- Approximate period: July 2021 to November 2021
- Peak: August 2021

Wave 4:
- Began in December 2021 but could not be fully evaluated within the selected analysis period.


## Cases and Death Trends

Cases and deaths generally increased during the same epidemic waves.

Visual inspection suggested that deaths may have followed increases in cases with a short delay during the second wave.

Additional analyses would be required to quantify the timing of this relationship.


## Wave Comparison

Wave 2 recorded the largest number of reported cases (97,643).

Wave 1 recorded the highest number of reported deaths (1,140).

Deaths per 1,000 reported cases were:

- Wave 1: 18.22
- Wave 2: 9.11
- Wave 3: 14.19

Wave 1 therefore exhibited the highest mortality burden relative to the number of reported cases.


## Interpretation

The relationship between reported cases and mortality differed across epidemic waves.

Although Wave 2 had the highest case burden, it exhibited a lower deaths-per-case ratio than Wave 1.

Potential explanations include:

- changes in testing practices,
- improvements in clinical management,
- population immunity,
- differences in circulating variants.

The available dataset does not permit determination of the exact cause.


## Limitations

- Daily case and death reporting became increasingly incomplete after 2021.
- Results are based on surveillance data and may be affected by under-reporting.
- Wave boundaries were identified through visual inspection and should be interpreted as approximate.
